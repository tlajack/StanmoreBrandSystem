Slipstream

Round three, and this one starts from the field: a livery spotted on the road — tapered ribbon strokes wrapping the bodywork in black and red. That vocabulary is worth stealing. It has the motion the client has wanted from day one, it layers two colours the way Stanmore already does, and it was born as cut vinyl, so production is native, not adapted.

Slipstream is that vocabulary rebuilt on Stanmore’s geometry: hard‑edged blade tapers instead of soft calligraphy, elbows that wrap the wordmark the way the reference wraps a window, and the dart cluster running at the wordmark’s slant.

The reference

What gets kept: the tapered ribbon that wraps a fixed object, the thin echo stroke in the second colour, and the detached dart cluster that gives the panel motion. What changes: the reference’s soft, calligraphic curves become straight runs with rounded elbows and blade‑pointed tips — pinstriping with Stanmore’s motorsport edge — and every angle in the dart cluster comes from the wordmark’s slant. Red and black only, on the white of the vehicle.

The element system

Bracket, echo, darts

Three components, always used together: a black bracket ribbon that wraps the wordmark’s leading corner, a red echo inside it, and a red‑over‑black dart cluster trailing off toward the direction of travel. The bracket gives the mark a home on any white surface — no red panel required — which is what finally lets the card and the van share one device.

Production. Every shape is a flat two‑colour cut — this direction is vinyl‑first by construction. Blade tips get trimmed in artwork at 3 mm so the plotter never chases a zero‑width point. The bracket, echo, and darts ship as one grouped asset with the logo position locked; in one colour, everything runs black.

Where this lands

The real decision is the ground, not the graphic. The full Slipstream system wants white bodywork — that is what makes the reference livery feel light and fast — which means the van moves from a solid red panel to a white‑dominant livery. If the client isn’t ready for that, the hybrid keeps the red panel exactly as it is and borrows only the sweep and darts, at the cost of some of the reference’s airiness.

Next step: confirm ground (white livery or hybrid), then the system gets cut as production SVGs — card back, van side both lengths, rear doors — plus the usage note for the standards doc.
